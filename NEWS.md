## Top row — 3 big illustrated stories

### 1. Three leaders, one table: Trump, Xi and Putin meet in Shenzhen
- The first-ever trilateral meeting of the leaders of the world's
  three largest nuclear powers, on the sidelines of the 33rd APEC
  leaders' meeting in Shenzhen on 19 Nov. Nuclear arms control is
  Trump's stated priority; Beijing resists making it central. Xi
  hugged Trump on the tarmac on arrival; the three posed for a group
  photo. Trump also holds a bilateral and working lunch with Xi and
  a one-on-one with Putin; the White House says Ukraine will not be
  raised in the trilateral.
- Sources: BusinessWorld 19 Nov 09:13; Reuters 18 Nov (Kremlin:
  one hour set aside for the three-way); Le Monde 19 Nov; Qazinform
  19 Nov 03:30.

### 2. GTA VI launches worldwide after a 13-year wait
- Grand Theft Auto VI launches 19 Nov at midnight local time on
  PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S ($79.99), after two delays
  (from 2025, then 26 May 2026). First reviews: 99/100 on
  Metacritic from 140+ reviews; 2.3 million watching on Twitch by
  midday in Paris. Pre-orders already past $1.5 billion (Sensor
  Tower). No PC version announced. Set in Leonida (fictional
  Florida), starring Jason Duval and Lucia Caminos.
- Sources: Le Monde 19 Nov; People 19 Nov; CNN 18 Nov; Desert Sun
  18 Nov; jagoamerica 19 Nov.

### 3. Anthropic's record $81.3B IPO debuts on Nasdaq
- Anthropic priced its IPO at $83 a share on 18 Nov, above the
  $70–78 range, and begins trading on Nasdaq on Thursday 19 Nov
  under ticker ANT. 980 million shares sold; the deal raises about
  $81.3 billion — the largest IPO on record, topping SpaceX's
  $75 billion in June — at a valuation of roughly $1.5 trillion.
  Six times oversubscribed; Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley lead
  a 22-bank syndicate. First major frontier AI lab to go public.
- Sources: PYMNTS 19 Nov 02:37 UTC; Reuters via Hindu BusinessLine
  19 Nov; Upstox 19 Nov; PaisaKawach 19 Nov.

### 6. Van Gogh sells for a record $190.4m
- Vincent van Gogh's "Chatagniers en fleurs" ("Blossoming Chestnut
  Trees", 1890) sold at Sotheby's New York for $190.4 million
  including fees — a new auction record for the artist, beating
  $117.2 million from 2022. Hammer $165 million; bidding lasted
  about three minutes; buyer is dealer Bill Acquavella. The sale
  went ahead a day after a settlement with the heirs of German
  publisher Samuel Fischer over its 1936 sale under Nazi pressure.
  (Hammer fell Wednesday evening New York time; reported by BBC at
  00:49 UTC 19 Nov.)
- Sources: BBC via NewsBites 19 Nov 00:49 UTC; Artnet 18 Nov
  23:25 UTC, updated 19 Nov 02:16 UTC; The Trent 19 Nov 00:57 UTC.
